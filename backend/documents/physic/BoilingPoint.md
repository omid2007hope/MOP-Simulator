# Boiling Point

The **boiling point** of a substance is the temperature at which the vapor pressure of a liquid equals the surrounding ambient pressure, causing the liquid phase to transition into a vapor state.

Under standard atmospheric pressure ($1\text{ atm} = 101.325\text{ kPa}$), this temperature is referred to as the *normal boiling point*.

## 📌 Technical Notes & Anomalies

> [!NOTE]
> **Symbols & Conventions:** $Z$ is the standard symbol for atomic number; $C_p$ is the standard symbol for heat capacity; and $\chi$ is the standard symbol for electronegativity on the Pauling scale.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **[1] Helium Solidification:** Helium does not solidify at standard atmospheric pressure ($1\text{ atm}$). It requires pressures above $25\text{ atm}$ to solidify, extending down to near absolute zero ($0\text{ K}$).

> [!WARNING]
> **[2] Carbon Sublimation:** Carbon does not melt under standard atmospheric pressure; instead, it sublimes directly from solid to gas at approximately $4,100\text{ K}$.

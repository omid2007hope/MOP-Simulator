Obliquity angle, also known as axial tilt, is the angle between an astronomical object's rotational axis and its orbital axis (the line perpendicular to its orbital plane). Equivalently, it is defined as the angle between the planet's equatorial plane and its orbital plane.

For Earth, this angle is currently approximately 23.4 degrees (specifically ~23.44° or 23°26'). This tilt is responsible for the planet's seasons, as it causes different hemispheres to receive varying amounts of solar radiation throughout the year. Over long timescales, Earth's obliquity oscillates between 22.1 and 24.5 degrees in a cycle lasting about 41,000 years.

Definition: The angle between the rotational axis and the orbital axis.
Earth's Current Value: ~23.4°.
Range: Oscillates between 22.1° and 24.5°.
Cycle Period: Approximately 41,000 years.
Effect: Determines the intensity and severity of seasons; higher tilt leads to more extreme seasonal contrasts.

A rotational axis is an imaginary line around which an object rotates, with all points of the rigid body moving in circles centered on this line. This axis can be located within the object (such as Earth's spin axis passing through its poles) or external to it (such as a planet orbiting the Sun).

Key characteristics include:

Center of Mass: The axis typically passes through the object's center of mass, or barycenter, where the object appears balanced.
Perpendicularity: The axis is perpendicular to the plane of motion for the rotating object.
Aviation Applications: In aircraft, rotation occurs around three perpendicular axes intersecting at the center of gravity: longitudinal (roll), lateral (pitch), and vertical (yaw).
Molecular Symmetry: In chemistry, a rotational axis ($C_n$) is defined by the number of distinct orientations ($n$) a molecule achieves that are indistinguishable from its original state upon rotation.

The term orbital axis has distinct definitions in astronomy and anatomy. In astronomy, it is the line perpendicular to the orbital plane (e.g., the ecliptic for Earth), serving as a reference for axial tilt or obliquity, which is the angle between a planet’s rotational axis and this orbital axis. In anatomy, the orbital axis is a line passing through the apex of the bony orbit (optic canal) and the center of the opening of the orbit, used for morphological analysis and coordinate systems.

Astronomical Context
The orbital axis is perpendicular to the plane in which a celestial body orbits its primary.
Earth's axial tilt (~23.4°) is the angle between Earth's rotational axis and its orbital axis.
